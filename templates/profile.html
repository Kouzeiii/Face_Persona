The 128×128 face image is divided into a 16×16 grid of cells (8px each). Each cell independently measures gradient directions — this is why it's non-linear: different regions (eyes, nose, mouth, jawline) contribute separate measurements. Darker cells have stronger gradients.

Step 3 — Gradient Directions (HOG)
For each cell, the Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG) measures edge directions in 9 orientations (0° to 160°). Each line shows the dominant gradient direction and strength. This captures facial features like edges of eyes, nose bridges, and lips as numerical values.
Orientations9 bins (0°–160°)

Step 4 — 512-Dimensional Vector
All gradient values are flattened into a 512-dimensional numerical vector and L2-normalized. This vector is the face's unique "numerical fingerprint" — fed into the ML pipeline below.
